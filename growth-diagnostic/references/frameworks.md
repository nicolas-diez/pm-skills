# Framing frameworks

## 1. The CFO's ABC (mother structure of the storytelling)

Three chapters, each with ONE question and ONE anchor number:
1. **How does this business make money?** Unit economics per transaction: revenue per order → cost waterfall → contribution margin. "Every order matters: your work shows up here."
2. **What is a customer worth?** LTV = frequency × active months × margin/order. Contrast two example customers (average vs high-frequency: in transactional businesses the gap is routinely 4x to 50x). Healthy LTV/CAC 3-5x; payback in months. The CFO's three levers: retention (more months), unit economics (more margin), acquisition efficiency (not every CAC is equal).
3. **Where does the next unit of budget go?** Portfolio per lifecycle stage with expected LTV/CAC, caps, cut rules. Profitable growth self-finances (working capital).
Close: value is created at every layer / customers are long-term bets / profitable growth funds the next experiment.

Storytelling rules: one anchor number per chapter; one named example customer instead of tables in the body; tables go to the annex; the executive summary opens with the recommendation.

## 2. Consulting-style deck building (problem solving / pyramid principle)

- **Dummy pack first:** sketch headline + key argument per slide BEFORE building anything.
- **The headline IS the message:** a claim with the key number inside, 1-2 lines, unique even without the chart, fits the story. Never generic labels ("Retention analysis" ✗ / "Frequency among actives is healthy; the bleed is converting new users into repeaters" ✓).
- **One message per table or chart.** Two messages = two charts or two slides.
- Structure: current situation → initiatives → conclusions.
- With audiences that don't know you yet, charts don't decorate: **they build the trust your reputation can't yet.**

## 3. Canonical growth/marketplace checklist (Andreessen Horowitz a16z 13 metrics · Jonathan Hsu/Social Capital growth accounting · Lenny marketplace metrics · Reforge cohorts · Gurley)

The 4 gotchas separating senior from junior in a marketplace case:
1. Mixing failure types (unfilled vs cancellation) as one problem.
2. **Ignoring the supply side** (explains most demand plateaus).
3. Full-period averages without a time series.
4. EDA without a prioritized, sized recommendation.

## 4. Company history before proposing

Before proposing any program (membership, loyalty, tier, referrals): research what the company tried before, what it killed, and what rails are live today. Proposing to "evolve the existing asset" with that history cited reads 10x more senior than pitching the generic idea.

## 5. Culture as a framing filter

If the case involves cross-team collaboration: read the company's public values (principles, job posts) and calibrate language. Safe framing: shared goal, clear ownership, "I bring the signal, not an imposed solution". Avoid invading verbs ("I will direct your area").
